# STORY_OPTIONS.md: three v4 concepts (the chosen one is developed in STORY.md)

Constraints every option had to meet: completely different from v3 (v3 = "Ocean lane vs Pond lane" forward supply chain with a start-screen choice, sand/deep-sea/rust palette, Instrument Serif + Inter Tight) and from the live skylarkexim.com (WordPress template); only facts from CONTENT.md; every visual built in Blender or live Three.js (no AI media, no stock); one continuous Zoi-style 3D journey (camera flies between scenes, pinned type, no dashboard UI); renderable overnight on one Mac.

## Option A — "Trace it back" (reverse journey, countdown 09 → 01)  ✅ CHOSEN
**Logline:** A carton of Skylark shrimp sits on a dinner table somewhere abroad. Each scroll rewinds its journey: reefer at sea → Vizag port → cold store → packing → freezing → quality lab → grading → peeling → washing → the chilled truck → the partner farm pond and the fishing boat at dawn, ending on the people. Then the site turns round: "Now, forward." — products, quality, markets, partners, contact.
**Device:** the site's own 9-step work flow (CONTENT.md §5) becomes a countdown. The giant numeral counts 09, 08 … 01 as the viewer travels upstream. One hero object (the Skylark master carton) is visible in every chapter, so the eye always knows what it is following.
**Why it wins:** (1) The structure IS the real content (9 verified steps), so no invented claims are needed to fill time. (2) It opens on the buyer's world (the people who will visit the site), not on a factory. (3) The reverse direction gives every transition a purpose — the camera always moves *upstream* — which makes one continuous camera flight natural (Zoi). (4) No path choice, no start screen (v3's dashboard start was the thing Tarun hated most). (5) Ends emotionally on people and the source, then flips to business at the moment of highest trust.
**Risks and answers:** reversed physics would look wrong (water running backwards) → every shot plays its action *forwards*; only the chapter order is reversed, and the camera flight between chapters moves upstream. Ten-plus scenes overnight → shared sets (one plant set gives 7 chapters), 960×540 previews first, full renders in priority order, preview-grade stop-gap so nothing is ever a placeholder.

## Option B — "One night of cold" (the cold chain as a single unbroken take)
**Logline:** Follow the temperature, not the product: a single 90-second camera move that never leaves the cold — from ice on the boat deck, into the chilled truck, the IQF tunnel, glazing, the −18 °C to −20 °C cold room, the sealed reefer — told as one night shift from dusk to dawn.
**Strengths:** visually unified (frost, ice, steam, blue-white light), very continuous, technically simpler (mostly interiors, few people).
**Why not chosen:** the site gives only one temperature fact (−18 °C to −20 °C), so the story's central device has no facts to carry it; long stretches of similar blue interiors would feel monotonous at award level; night/blue lighting risks the "dark overlay / murky" failure Tarun rejected in v3 (G-LIGHT gates).

## Option C — "Hands" (told only through the hands at each step)
**Logline:** Macro shots of hands — the farmer lifting a net, the crew sorting on deck, gloved hands peeling, grading, sealing a carton, a buyer's hand opening it — cut on matching hand gestures.
**Strengths:** intimate, human, distinctive.
**Why not chosen:** hands and fingers are the hardest thing to make convincing in Blender overnight (v3 failed on mannequin poses and "floating hands"); macro framing hides the scale of the operation (port, plant, 700-pallet warehouse) that buyers want to see. Its best idea — ending on the people — is kept in Option A's final chapter, at mid-distance with backlight so it stays believable.

## Rejected outright
- Any "two lanes / choose your path" structure (too close to v3).
- A 3D globe finale (v3 used it): v4 shows markets on a Blender-rendered relief map with drawn routes instead.
